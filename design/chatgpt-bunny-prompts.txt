PETAL BUNNY CHARACTER PROMPTS

============================================================
PROMPT 1 — CREATE A STYLE COMPARISON
============================================================

Upload both original rabbit reference photos, then paste this prompt:

Create an original character-design comparison sheet for a mobile calorie-tracking app.

Use the uploaded rabbit photos only as high-level visual inspiration for recognizable rabbit anatomy and traits: floppy lop ears, a compact round body, tiny front paws, cocoa-brown and cream fur, soft cheeks, and a small pink nose. Do not trace, copy, composite, or directly edit the reference photos.

Show three clearly labeled art directions:

1. Polished pixel art — a detailed modern game sprite, approximately 128×128 in appearance, with clean pixel clusters. It must not look blocky, geometric, or abstract.

2. Cute low-poly 3D — rounded plush proportions, tasteful faceted forms, dimensional fur shapes, and soft studio lighting.

3. Kawaii illustrated sticker — smooth shapes, an expressive face, a clean dark-brown outline, and polished mobile-app mascot quality.

For each direction, show the same recognizable lop-eared bunny character in three states:

SLEEPY:
Sitting or curled up with a drooping posture, relaxed floppy ears, half-closed eyes, and an obviously tired expression.

STRONG:
Standing upright and looking confident and energetic, adorably flexing its front paws. It should look strong while remaining cute and unmistakably rabbit-like.

SAD / OVERFULL:
A slumped posture, downturned expression, drooping ears, and a slightly overstuffed tummy. The expression should feel sympathetic and gentle rather than distressed.

Keep the character’s markings, proportions, colors, facial features, and lop-ear shape consistent across all nine designs.

Use a warm cocoa-brown, cream, dusty-pink, and dark-chocolate palette suitable for a dark-mode app.

Every design must immediately read as a real floppy-eared rabbit. It must not resemble a bear, cat, blob, human, or generic fantasy creature.

Present the nine concepts in a clean 3-column × 3-row comparison sheet, with style names across the top and mood names along the side.

Use a plain neutral background. Do not include an app interface, scenery, food, logos, accessories, or text other than the style and mood labels. Avoid photorealism.


============================================================
PROMPT 2 — CREATE THE FINAL PRODUCTION SPRITES
============================================================

After choosing a direction from the comparison sheet, paste this prompt:

Using option [INSERT OPTION NUMBER OR STYLE NAME] from the previous comparison, create a production-ready sprite sheet of that exact original bunny character.

Include three poses:

1. Sleepy
2. Strong
3. Sad / overfull

Preserve identical fur markings, cocoa-and-cream palette, body proportions, floppy-ear shape, facial features, line treatment, lighting, and rendering style across all three sprites.

The poses must remain clearly different:

- Sleepy: low energy, drooping posture, relaxed ears, and half-closed eyes.
- Strong: upright, confident, energetic, and adorably flexing its front paws.
- Sad / overfull: slumped, drooping ears, sympathetic expression, and a slightly full tummy.

Center each sprite inside an equal square cell with generous and consistent padding.

Use a genuinely transparent background. Include no labels, borders, scenery, food, logos, accessories, floor, or shadows extending outside the sprite cells.

Keep the full character visible in every cell. Do not crop ears, paws, or body parts.

Make the three sprites large, crisp, and high resolution for use in a mobile web application. Maintain clear readability when each sprite is displayed at approximately 160×160 pixels.

Output the sprite sheet as a PNG with transparency.
